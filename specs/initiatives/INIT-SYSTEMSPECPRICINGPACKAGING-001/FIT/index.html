    systemspec-pricingpackaging: Fit of the Model to Three Product Shapes

    Initiative: INIT-SYSTEMSPECPRICINGPACKAGING-001 Work item: RMI-SYSTEMSPECPRICINGPACKAGING-001

    The model in the TRD was written from one product's plans. This record checks it against three invented shapes that between them cover what the first release must express, and lists what did not fit and how the model changes. The shapes are composites; none is a real product.

    The three shapes

    A. A tools suite for small teams. Three applications behind one sign-in. Tiers Free, Builder, Team and an arranged Enterprise. Limits are counts (sites, pages) and a daily rate; one feature is a ladder (refresh: daily, hourly). Seats differ by tier. One platform account, several applications.

    B. A course platform for creators. Tiers Free, Creator, Business. Many differences are levels, not switches: branding (required, optional, removed), quiz and analytics depth, certificate styling, API access (none, limited, full). Course count is a limit, "students" is advertised as unlimited with a fair-use policy, and certificates issued per month is a fair-use allowance. Creators sell courses to learners and the platform takes a fee; selling is unavailable on Free. Video hosting differs by tier as a set of providers.

    C. A family learning app. A free tier that nobody pays for, a family subscription paid by a parent, a one-time purchase that issues a verified credential, a marketplace where creators set prices and the platform takes a share, and an arranged institution licence. Rules say what payment may never buy.

    What fit

    Need In the model
    Free, monthly and yearly plans; arranged plans Plan with Prices, SelfServe
    Counts with explicit unlimited LimitValue with Unlimited
    A daily or monthly allowance Limit.Period
    A ladder of levels (refresh, branding, quiz and analytics depth, certificate style, API access) Feature.Levels, ordered; unset is off
    A switch (custom domain, selling, team management) A feature without levels
    A trial Plan.TrialDays
    A one-time purchase with a fixed price Product
    A seller-set price with a floor Product.SellerSet, MinAmountCents
    A marketplace share Marketplace.PlatformFeeBps
    Several applications behind one account Grants per tier and application
    Seats differing by tier TierGrant.Seats or UnlimitedSeats

    What did not fit, and the change

    1. The marketplace fee can differ by plan. In shape B a creator on a higher plan may pay a lower share. A single Marketplace.PlatformFeeBps cannot say that. Change: Plan.PlatformFeeBps is an optional override of the marketplace default.
    2. Advertised "unlimited" with a fair-use policy. In B, students are unlimited but subject to anti-abuse limits. Unlimited and a numeric fair-use threshold are different things. Change: LimitValue.FairUse may accompany Unlimited; then it means "advertised as unlimited, subject to the host's fair-use policy", and Max is ignored. With a Max, FairUse means crossing it warns and flags rather than refuses.
    3. A set of providers is not a ladder. B's video hosting is "one provider", then "that one or an external one", then "external or integrated". Where the sets are nested this is a ladder, but it need not be. Change: the rule is that levels are only for monotonic ladders; a capability that is a set (video providers, payment methods) is several features without levels. A lint warns when a levelled feature's levels read as names of independent options.
    4. Applications were an undeclared string. A grant named an application with nothing to check it against. Change: the document declares Applications, and grants refer to them. This closes the open question in the TRD.
    5. Seats had two homes. A plan limit and a tier grant could both say how many members. Change: seats are in grants only. A product that wants a member cap as a limit expresses it as a grant; there is one place to enforce it.
    6. A one-time product that issues something. In C a purchase issues a verified credential. The model records a price, not what the purchase gives. Decision: what a product grants is the host's, identified by the product ID it receives when the purchase completes. The spec stays a description of the offer, not of fulfilment. Recorded as out of scope for the first release.
    7. Who may pay, and what payment may never buy. C's rules (a parent pays, payment never buys mastery) are policy. Decision: policy stays with the host. A feature may carry an advisory Category in a later release so a lint can flag a paid-only feature in a category the product has promised to keep free; it is not in the first release.
    8. Audience. Pricing pages group plans for individuals, families, teams and institutions. Decision: deferred; a page can group by tier.

    Result

    Two additions to the types (Plan.PlatformFeeBps, Applications), one change of meaning (FairUse with Unlimited), and two rules (levels only for ladders; seats only in grants). Three things are recorded as out of scope for the first release: what a product grants, payer policy, and audience. The model otherwise holds for all three shapes without extension.
